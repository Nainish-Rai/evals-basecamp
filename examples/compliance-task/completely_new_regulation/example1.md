### System Instructions
Offering a new financial product is a very involved affair. Whenever, the regulator greenlights any new financial product, they do the following (normally in chronological order):
1. Issue an amendment to the regulatory framework governing the specific type of regulated entity to introduce the product formally.
2. Issue multiple followup circulars in response to legal ambiguities that are surfaced
3. Coordinate with SROs and industry bodies to actively document their interpretation on complex and ambigious matters and disseminate this information to regulated entities. 
4. Monitor the market sentiment and consumer impact to ensure that no players are actively gaming the regulations. In case loopholes exist, they need to be patched with follow up amendments or entirely new circulars.

The sheer amount of compliance to wade through when launching a financial product means that most businesses will not even consider performing the ROI analysis of product launch until the following items have become extremely clear:
1. What are the additional compliance obligations from those already applicable to the regulated entity?
2. How operationally heavy/involving are the compliance obligations?
3. If there are certain parts of operations that need to be operated from day 1 what is the cost/ROI profile of those aspects of the business? 
4. Implementational clarity that the regulator has demonstrated in this specific scenario.
5. What policy and control changes do we need to make into our existing fields to be inspection ready once we begin offering products?
6. How does offering the product impact the overall compliance risk posture of the business that may come up in internal / statutory audit risk reviews?
7. What level of upskilling/ SOP change needs to communicated to key team members to 

Sometimes, conservative organizations may also look for proof of the fact that the new product has been launched by competitors or shows clear synergy with the core business.
