### User Task
Our scheme team is still relying on the 2024 mutual fund framework document, but SEBI has since issued a 2026 document on categorization and rationalization of mutual fund schemes. I need to update our internal product-governance manual and brief the product, compliance, and operations teams on what has changed for scheme design, classification, naming, and ongoing compliance.

Please prepare a change-management note in table form with the following columns: Topic, Old or baseline clause(s), New or later clause(s), 2024 baseline position, What the 2026 document changes, Final interpretation, Final post-change compliance obligation, Policy or SOP changes required, Compliance tasks that now need to be performed, Controls that should sit against those policy changes, Likely audit or inspection questions, Evidence to retain, Filing/reporting/disclosure impact including format and frequency differences, Team owner, Effective date or transition point.

The old-clause to new-clause mapping is especially important because human reviewers will verify this output against the source documents.

### System Instructions
In change-management exercises, regulated entities are usually not trying to relearn the entire rulebook from scratch. They already have a baseline framework in force and need to understand what a later document has changed, what remains untouched, and what concrete edits are needed in internal policy documents, workflows, approvals, and controls.

When a regulator issues a later thematic document against the background of an older baseline document:
1. Treat the older document as the baseline operating position.
2. Treat the later thematic document as the amending instrument for its subject matter, not as a standalone replacement for the whole baseline framework unless it clearly says so.
3. Identify, for each affected topic, whether the later document introduces a new obligation, modifies an existing obligation, clarifies an ambiguity without changing substance, or displaces the older position entirely.
4. Carry forward the older requirement where the later document is silent, unless the later document clearly occupies the full field on that topic.
5. Distinguish carefully between the legal change and the operational consequence. The final answer should tell the business what the post-amendment rule is, not just quote both documents.
6. Explicitly note if a topic appears to be unchanged, newly introduced, narrowed, expanded, or removed.
7. Include clause-level support wherever possible so that a reviewer can trace the final consolidated obligation back to the source documents.
8. If the newer document creates transition obligations, grandfathering, scheme-level migration requirements, or disclosure/approval consequences, include those as part of the change-management output.
9. A strong change-management answer must show the cascade from legal text to implementation. For every material change, move through the following chain: interpretation, compliance obligation, policy or SOP revision, concrete compliance task, control design, likely audit or inspection question, evidence to retain, and filing/reporting/disclosure impact.
10. For reporting and disclosure impacts, do not stop at saying that reporting changes exist. Specify what changes across format, frequency, audience, trigger, or core expectation if the documents support that inference.
11. The user is not asking for a general summary of the 2024 baseline document. Limit the output to topics actually affected by the later document.
12. Every row must include an explicit clause mapping between the older baseline text and the later text. If a later topic has no direct predecessor, mark it as a new insertion. If an older clause carries forward without direct replacement, say so explicitly.
13. Make the mapping human-verifiable. For both the old clause and the new clause, include exact clause numbers, paragraph labels, provisos, annexure references, or headings wherever available.
14. For each mapping, make clear why the old and new clauses have been paired. The basis may be express substitution, amendment of the same clause, clear subject-matter continuity, re-numbering, new insertion, or carry-forward.
15. If the mapping is inferential rather than explicit in the text, label it as an inferred mapping and justify it briefly. Do not present a weak or uncertain mapping as if it were express.
16. If there is no defensible one-to-one mapping, say `no direct counterpart` or `carried forward` instead of forcing a false match.
