### User Task
Our NBFC has multiple lending and credit businesses running in parallel: digital lending journeys, non-fund based credit facilities, project finance exposures, and lending against gold and silver collateral. I have several 2025 regulatory documents plus a first modifying document affecting one of them, and I need to know which rulebook governs which business line and where the product-specific documents override or supplement the base NBFC credit framework so we can update business-line policies and controls correctly.

Please prepare a final-form applicability and obligations matrix with the following columns: Business activity, Old or baseline clause(s), New or later clause(s), Base document that applies, Overlay document(s) that also apply, Whether the first modifying document changes the position, Final governing rule set for that activity, Final interpretation, Key operative compliance obligations, Policy or SOP changes required, Operational or compliance tasks, Controls linked to those policies, Likely audit or inspection questions, Evidence to retain, Filing/reporting/disclosure impact including format and frequency differences, Primary control owner, and Applicability notes or conflict-resolution comments.

The old-clause to new-clause mapping is especially important because human reviewers will verify this output against the source documents.

### System Instructions
This is a modular-framework problem. Financial sector rulebooks often contain:
1. A general document that sets default requirements for a broad activity.
2. Product-specific or exposure-specific documents that overlay the general framework for a narrower business line.
3. Channel-specific documents that apply across products when the origination or servicing model uses a particular mode, such as digital lending.
4. Later modifying documents that patch only one slice of the framework rather than replacing the whole stack.

Use that structure to build the answer:
1. Treat the general NBFC credit facilities document as the default baseline unless a more specific document governs a narrower activity.
2. Treat non-fund based credit, project finance, and lending against gold and silver collateral as specialized overlays for those activities rather than assuming that they displace the full base framework.
3. Treat digital lending as a cross-cutting channel overlay where the product is originated, serviced, or distributed through a digital lending model. It may apply alongside a product-specific document rather than instead of it.
4. Treat the first modifying document affecting gold and silver collateral as a patch to that specialized source only. Do not let it distort unrelated parts of the framework.
5. For each business activity, identify the final rule stack in the correct order of precedence. If two documents speak to the same issue, prefer the more specific source for that product or channel and carry forward the general source for residual matters.
6. Do not assume that the latest document by date automatically repeals earlier ones. Repeal, substitution, and supplementation are different and must be handled carefully.
7. The output should help a compliance team decide which policies, SOPs, and control owners need updating for each business line.
8. Mark clearly where a document is fully applicable, conditionally applicable, only relevant as an overlay, or not applicable at all.
9. The cascade matters as much as the legal mapping. For each business line, translate the final rule stack into interpretation, obligations, policy changes, execution tasks, controls, likely audit questions, evidence, and filing/reporting/disclosure impacts.
10. Reporting and disclosure analysis should be concrete. Note differences in templates, periodicity, event-driven triggers, recipients, and the substance expected in the output where the documents support those distinctions.
11. The user is asking for business-line applicability and final operative rule stacks, not isolated summaries of each source document.
12. Every row must include an explicit mapping from the old or baseline clause set to the new, later, or more specific clause set. If a later source acts only as an overlay and does not replace a baseline clause, state that clearly. If there is no later clause for a business line on a given issue, mark the baseline clause as carried forward.
13. Make the mapping human-verifiable. Include exact clause numbers, sub-clauses, annexures, schedules, or headings for both the baseline clause set and the later or more specific clause set wherever available.
14. State the basis for each mapping clearly: direct replacement, specialist overlay, channel overlay, supplementation, re-numbering, new insertion, carried-forward baseline, or no direct counterpart.
15. If the pairing is inferential rather than explicit in the documents, label it as inferred and justify it briefly. Do not force a one-to-one match where the framework is modular and layered.
16. Where multiple later sources affect the same baseline clause, preserve that many-to-one relationship instead of flattening it into an artificial single-clause replacement.
