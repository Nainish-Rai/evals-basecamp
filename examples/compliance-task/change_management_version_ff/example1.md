### User Task
Our NBFC compliance manual is being refreshed using a 2025-updated baseline framework document, but the team has also received a separate 2025 asset liability management document. I need a single source-of-truth note for the risk, treasury, and compliance teams that tells me whether the ALM document merely restates the updated framework, supplements it, or overrides it on specific topics.

Please prepare a final-form compliance matrix with the following columns: Topic, Old or baseline clause(s), New or later clause(s), Does the updated baseline document already cover it, What the standalone ALM document does to that topic, Final governing source, Final interpretation, Final operative compliance obligation, Policy or framework changes required, Operational or compliance tasks, Controls linked to those policy changes, Likely audit or inspection questions, Evidence to retain, Filing/reporting/disclosure impact including format and frequency differences, Primary owner inside the NBFC, and Notes on implementation.

The old-clause to new-clause mapping is especially important because human reviewers will verify this output against the source documents.

### System Instructions
Versioned regulatory frameworks often create a false sense that every document is additive. In practice, one document may be a consolidated omnibus framework, while another is a later specialist rulebook for the same subject. The task is to determine the final operative position as of the latest version, not to produce parallel summaries of overlapping text.

Apply the following reasoning:
1. Treat the updated baseline document as a consolidated framework that may already absorb prior amendments or cross-refer to topic-specific sources.
2. Treat the standalone ALM document as a potentially more specific and later rule source for ALM topics, but do not assume that it repeals the broader baseline framework unless it says so expressly or by necessary implication.
3. For each overlapping topic, determine whether the ALM document restates, supplements, replaces, narrows, or expands what appears in the updated baseline document.
4. If the same obligation appears in both documents, do not count it twice. Cite the document that is more specific or more current as the final governing source.
5. Preserve broader governance, definitional, escalation, and supervisory context from the omnibus framework where the specialist document does not displace it.
6. Focus on the final operative rule for the NBFC, especially for board oversight, ALCO responsibilities, treasury controls, risk monitoring, and regulatory reporting.
7. Tag each topic conceptually as carried forward, supplemented, replaced, or newly introduced so that the output is useful for policy maintenance.
8. The final answer should read like a source-of-truth consolidation memo for change management, not a historical chronology.
9. Change management here must be downstream and implementation-facing. For each topic, carry the analysis through interpretation, obligation, policy revision, tasks, controls, audit or inspection questions, evidence, and filing/reporting/disclosure consequences.
10. If two documents lead to a different reporting, disclosure, or escalation expectation, state the practical difference in cadence, form, trigger, or destination instead of collapsing them into a generic note.
11. The user wants the latest operative position for each ALM-related area. Avoid double counting overlapping text or presenting parallel summaries where one final governing source can be identified.
12. Every row must show the clause mapping from the baseline text to the later or more specific text. If the later document only supplements the baseline without replacing a clause, say that explicitly. If the baseline continues unchanged, identify the carry-forward clause.
13. Make the mapping human-verifiable. Include exact clause numbers, sub-clauses, schedules, annexures, or headings for both the baseline text and the later or more specific text wherever available.
14. State the basis for each mapping clearly: direct overlap, supplementation, replacement, re-numbering, narrower specialist overlay, broader baseline carry-forward, or no direct counterpart.
15. If the relationship between the two texts is inferential rather than explicit, label it as inferred and justify the pairing briefly. Do not overstate confidence where the documents are only thematically aligned.
16. If a later or more specific clause does not replace the baseline clause but merely sits on top of it, preserve both positions and explain that overlay rather than forcing a false old-to-new substitution.
